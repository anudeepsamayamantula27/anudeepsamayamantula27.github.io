My hypothesis for the Sunrisers

From watched to valued.

The Orange Army watches like a top-four fan base. That's an opportunity: turn more of those viewers into year-round fans, and SRH's value can catch up.

My hypothesis. Part of the difference is history: CSK has five titles, SRH one. Part may be fixable: between IPL seasons, I assumed Hyderabad fans had little to follow, even though the group also plays in SA20 and The Hundred. So I tested it.

What 246 SRH fans told me · my survey, 23–26 Nov 2026

  • 98%already knew about Sunrisers Eastern Cape and Leeds, and 67% follow them regularly
  • 37%have never watched SRH at the home stadium. Getting tickets was the most-named problem
  • 75%own no official SRH merch: 43% own none and 32% only unofficial copies
  • 61%of the 141 living in Hyderabad rated a local watch-along for Eastern Cape or Leeds 4 or 5 out of 5
  • 54%would pay for a yearly fan membership, most of them up to ₹500

What changed my mind. I was wrong about awareness: these fans already follow all three teams, on their phones. The gap is turning that loyalty into something SRH can count: from the outside, I found little that brings Hyderabad fans together between seasons (the fan meet I found was in March, as the IPL began), many have never made it to the stadium, and few own official merch. That is a revenue gap, and it is the one One Orange Army is built for.

What I'd test next. These are engaged fans reached through SRH fan accounts on X, so next I'm surveying casual fans on Reddit. Then, with SRH's own data: off-season app and social activity, repeat ticket and merchandise buyers, and sign-ups from the small pilot below. If sign-ups miss their target, I'd drop the idea.

Sources: viewers, The Sunday Guardian, 9 May 2026 · brand values, Houlihan Lokey 2026 via CricTracker, 30 Jul 2026 · survey: my own, 339 real responses (246 SRH fans) via SRH fan accounts on X and r/SunrisersHyderabad, about 20,000 automated entries excluded; "would go" and "would pay" answers overstate real behaviour
